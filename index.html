03What the Budget gives back

For your housing type and income band, the headline support package is roughly the following each year:

  • CDC vouchers — S$400, household-level, redeemable at supermarkets and hawkers.
  • GSTV + U-Save — about S$880 combined; helps with utilities and offsets GST for lower-income households.

05What ten years does

Two things move over time: prices rise and the Budget grows. At 3.0% annual inflation and 1.0% annual subsidy growth, the two curves separate — and because each year's rise compounds on the previous one, the gap widens faster than the rates alone suggest. A 3% rate doesn't add 30% after a decade; it adds about 34%.

07The model, if you want it

The whole page is four short equations. Everything else is a way of seeing them.

Show the maths

Expenditure and support both compound annually:

E(n) = E(0) · (1 + i)n
S(n) = S(0) · (1 + s)n

The net burden is their difference, and effectiveness is the ratio:

B(n) = E(n) − S(n)
reduction(n) = 100 · S(n) / E(n)

Year-on-year change in burden:

ΔB(n) = E(0)(1+i)n−1 · i − S(0)(1+s)n−1 · s

The threshold year — the first n at which reduction drops below τ — has a closed form when inflation outruns subsidy growth (i > s):

n* = ⌈ ln(τ · E(0) / (100 · S(0))) / ln((1+s)/(1+i)) ⌉

When i ≤ s, the ratio S(n)/E(n) is non-decreasing and no such year exists.
